GPS, taken apart

Your phone needs a fourth satellite because it doesn’t know what time it is.

GPS satellites don’t tell your phone where it is. They tell it the time, and the phone’s own clock isn’t good enough to use it. Follow the four steps below (about two minutes) to see how it gets round that.

Why the real world needs four

In space, each distance puts you on a sphere around a satellite. With a wrong clock, three spheres leave a whole curve of places that fit. The fourth sphere crosses that curve at one point: you.

What is real on this page

The space view is a flat slice through the Earth and the GPS orbits, drawn to scale: Earth’s radius is 6,371 km and the orbits are 26,560 km from its centre. Satellites below your horizon can’t be heard, just as in reality. The circles, the crossing points, the curve of possible places and the chart are all computed as you move things.

“Solve” runs the same method real receivers use (Gauss–Newton least squares). It starts at the centre of the Earth with no idea where you are, and it is given only the satellites’ positions and the measured travel times, never where you really are.

What is simplified

A flat slice has one fewer dimension, so here it takes 3 satellites instead of 4. The Earth is a perfect sphere, the satellites hold still while you look, and their clocks are perfect. Real signals are also delayed by the air and bounce off buildings. The labs add those back in one at a time.
