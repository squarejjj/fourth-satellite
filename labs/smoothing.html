Lab 6 · Smoothing

Your phone doesn’t believe every fix

Each GPS fix jumps around by a few metres. But you didn’t teleport, so where you were a second ago, and how fast you were going, say roughly where you must be now. A Kalman filter keeps both opinions and blends them. Press Predict and Correct to step through a walk, or press Play.

Left: “I walk smoothly” (smooth but slow to notice turns). Right: “I could do anything” (follows every jump).

How it works

The filter tracks four numbers, east and north position and velocity, plus how unsure it is of them (the ellipse). Every second it does two things:

predict: position += velocity × 1 s uncertainty grows (by Q, set by “trust in the motion model”) correct: estimate += K × (fix − predicted position) uncertainty shrinks K = P / (P + R): its own uncertainty compared with the fix’s (R = jumpiness²)

If the fixes are very jumpy, K is small and the filter leans on its prediction. If you keep surprising it, Q is big, K grows and it leans on the fixes. With no fix (the tunnel) it can only predict, and the ellipse keeps growing until fixes return. The velocity is never measured directly; the filter learns it because position and velocity uncertainties are linked.

What we simplified

This is the generic textbook constant-velocity filter fed with finished fixes (“loosely coupled”), with independent 2-D noise on each fix. We found no source saying which filter any particular phone runs; real phones also mix in motion sensors and feed raw measurements into the filter. The walk and the noise are generated from a fixed seed.
